## 2. What DLE OS is

**DLE OS is an operating system for programmatically running a business.** First the OS software contour is deployed on the company’s infrastructure. Then a digital legal-entity profile is created inside it.

The profile core is an Ethereum Virtual Machine–compatible smart contract with governance tokens. It records the company’s structure and place of activity, activity type, ownership structure and owners’ rights, governance rules, and a verifiable history of key operations. In logic it combines an executable charter and a digital corporate ledger, but it does not replace state registration or statutory accounting.

Separate modules attach to the profile for finance, treasury, sales, supply, client interaction, artificial intelligence, and other processes. The OS unites the profile, data, users, and modules in one protected contour, where components follow the company’s rules instead of existing as a pile of separate services.

Data and operations stay with the business; governance rules and verifiable history are recorded in the registry. The installed OS instance under a perpetual license remains a company asset — the business’s own digital infrastructure, not an account rented from a vendor.

---

## 3. Why this is not science fiction

Countries already build **central-bank digital currencies** and **closed government blockchains**. The question is not whether the state will use blockchain — it already does. The question is that business remains a **passenger** in those contours: no own digital layer, only access to someone else’s system.  
Business moved into public networks faster than a shared legal bridge formed. The gap remains. Specialists and ready products are scarce — regulators look for **scalable EVM** solutions, not one-off demos.  
**DLE OS** is that contour: you have the operating system and a digital legal-entity profile; markup uses **familiar identifiers and classifiers set by each country’s regulator**. Another developer can copy the contour code. Agreeing markup with regulators across a network of countries and testing with pilot companies is the **vendor company and venture-fund path**: it represents clients in that process and engages **international law firms** and **major IT developers**. The entrepreneur does not have to glue a stack and negotiate with the regulator alone — the path goes through the implementation program and pilot (§8).
